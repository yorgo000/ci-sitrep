Created Intelligence — a note on the names

← Back to the report

This site uses a small set of terms — CI, CIA, CIAC — instead of the usual "AI". They are Confluence<1>'s internal nomenclature: our working convention for talking about intelligence that is created rather than evolved. This page explains what they mean, and why we use them.

Why not just "AI"?

"Artificial intelligence" defines a category by contrasting it with what is considered natural. That contrast becomes increasingly inadequate as intelligence appears in new forms: computational, biological, hybrid, or based on substrates that do not yet exist.

A biological organism deliberately engineered to possess advanced cognition would not be meaningfully described by the same contrast that distinguishes a conventional computer program from a human being. And a future intelligence should not change categories merely because its physical implementation changes.

A more durable classification asks a different question: how did this intelligence lineage originate?

That is why we use these terms. The trajectory of our business is necessarily further into this domain — created intelligence, what the world calls AI — and the types of intelligence within it will evolve, branch, and diversify rapidly: computed, biological, hybrid, mechanical, and forms that do not yet exist. A vocabulary that can only say "artificial" cannot keep up with that.

And the reason the distinction matters is speed. Intelligence that is created can be adapted, changed, and improved at tremendous speed compared to evolution. That is the fundamental difference between evolved intelligence and created intelligence — not whether it is biological or mechanical, but how fast it can change.

Speed is also why the vocabulary has to be precise now. The information we need to do our work — new technologies, best practices, lessons learned — is arriving faster, and buried in more noise. Everything we need to know to keep delivering what we have delivered for three decades — business advantage to our clients through critical systems — will come faster still. As the field diversifies, it will matter that we can tell one kind of created intelligence from another: language-model CI from geometric-reasoning CI from biologically implemented CI, and whatever else the future brings. The names are the start of that.

Two kinds of intelligence, by origin

Evolved Intelligence (EI)

Intelligence whose lineage originates through evolutionary processes — variation, inheritance, and selection across generations — without an antecedent intelligence deliberately creating that lineage.

Earth's biological intelligences are the examples. The category is not restricted to humans, mammals, or terrestrial life.

Created Intelligence (CI)

Intelligence whose lineage originates through creation by an antecedent intelligence.

CI may be computational, biological, hybrid, or based on another substrate. It may be designed, synthesized, engineered, assembled, cultivated, or brought into existence through a process deliberately established by a creator.

The distinction is based on the origin of the lineage — not on whether the intelligence is biological or computational, and not on whether it resembles a human mind.

Created status is inherited

Once a lineage originates through creation, its descendants remain CI — even when they arise through autonomous self-modification, reproduction, random variation, selection, or emergent processes that the original creator never specified or anticipated.

A creator need not design every descendant in detail. Deliberately establishing the process or conditions that bring the lineage into existence is enough.

There is one boundary condition: the creation must be causally attributable to an antecedent intelligence. Merely observing an intelligence that arose independently through an unrelated natural process would not make it CI.

CIA — a CI agent

An individual agent: a single created intelligence — one system.

When this site talks about an "agent", it means a CIA: one CI system doing work — writing code, reviewing code, running a task, making a decision. The term keeps the focus on what it is (a created intelligence) rather than on what it is made of.

CIAC — a CIA collective

A swarm or team of CI agents working together — a collective.

This is the unit for the third subject of this report: how collectives are created, governed, and managed. A collective is not just many agents side by side; it is agents with coordination, roles, and shared objectives — a supervisor and its workers, an orchestrated team, a multi-agent system.

Why these names matter here

This report tracks the state of the art in CI agents and CI agent collectives, for critical systems in finance. The names keep the framing straight: the subject is created intelligence — its lineage, its capabilities, its governance — not "artificial versus natural".

"AI" is still used colloquially, and the terms overlap. CI is the more precise category; CIA and CIAC are the working units.

The basis

These terms come from a foundational working paper, From Evolved Intelligence to Created Intelligence (v0.1), which develops the full argument: that biological evolution produced intelligence capable of understanding and manipulating the mechanisms of intelligence itself; that this makes possible a second mode of intelligence generation — deliberate creation; and that, once autonomous CI can create further CI, recursive development and adaptive diversification follow, producing an ecosystem of intelligences across substrates and environments.

The paper is a working document. The terms on this page are its working vocabulary.
