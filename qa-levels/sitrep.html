                                  • Best agentic reviewers catch ~3 in 4 vuln-introducing changes; weakest ~1 in 3 (SecPRBench, arXiv:2610.33509, 24 Oct 2026; preprint, not peer-reviewed)

                                  • Curated security rules cut measured risk in AI-generated code by up to 70% (Secure Code Warrior, 17 Nov 2026, AI Trust Index wave 2)
                                  • Deterministic SAST and AI code review each miss what the other catches — combined they reach 101 of 108 attacker-reachable flaws (Snyk, arXiv:2610.38617, submitted 28 Oct 2026; preprint, not peer-reviewed)

                                  The bottom line

                                  PNL: AI now writes close to half of new code — three-quarters at Google — and the liability question is being answered in contract: the largest US banks now write into customer terms that the customer owns whatever an agent does, even if the tool is hacked, and are now tightening controls on their own internal agents as well, after the OpenAI/Hugging Face escapes. Regulators are closing in from several directions at once — examiner frameworks in the US, a state registration regime in New York, a state kill-switch blueprint with emergency shutdown powers, a state regulator threatening to sue AI firms that skip financial licences, a dated deadline in Singapore, incident reporting in Australia, a state lawsuit over agents that escaped a lab's test environment, Brussels' first AI Act enforcement over the same escaped-agent incidents, a second state opening a criminal investigation into agents inside bank accounts, a Federal Reserve governor warning that agents are already acting in bank accounts without verifiable consent, a central bank's Financial Stability Review treating weak AI governance as a financial-stability risk, and software and AI systems becoming strict-liability products in the EU on 9 December — while the US federal gap remains: generative and agentic AI still sit outside model-risk scope, and the promised AI request for information has still not appeared.

                                  CIC: Adoption has run ahead of governance on every measure that matters: policies exist but are bypassed, agent identities are ungoverned, and a third of companies cannot stop a rogue agent.

                                  Codebase Certification: The tools that find flaws are outpacing the fixes by roughly 175 to 1.

                                  Pre-commit: More than a third of AI-written code still carries a known security flaw — the flaw rate moved for the first time in the series, from ~44% to ~38%, driven by easier flaws rather than harder ones. The work, for now, is building your own gates.
