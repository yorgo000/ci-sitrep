                                      • 55.6% passed every test while a working exploit succeeded (A.S.E 2.0 / CASE-Bench, arXiv:2611.06294, submitted 4 Nov 2026; preprint, not peer-reviewed)
                                      • Best agent 23.8% correct-and-secure; 23% correct but exploitable (SecureVibeBench, Chen et al., arXiv:2509.22097; ACL 2026 long paper, July 2026 — peer-reviewed)
                                      • A post-hoc review lifts working-and-secure to 47–56% at ~2.5× cost (SusVibes re-run, independent research commissioned by Checkmarx, reported 2 Nov 2026) — [VENDOR-COMMISSIONED]
                                      • 45–70% of working agent solutions bring the original vulnerability back (BugLedger Bench, results 2 Nov 2026)
                                      • Best agentic reviewers catch ~3 in 4 vuln-introducing changes; weakest ~1 in 3 (SecPRBench, arXiv:2610.33509, 24 Oct 2026; preprint, not peer-reviewed)
                                      • AI-generated patches introduce ~11× the vulnerabilities of developer patches (Sajadi et al., SWE-bench study, v4, 1 Nov 2026; preprint)
                                      • Best agent writes working and secure code on only 25.1% of tasks (LSCBench, arXiv:2610.14766, submitted 10 Oct 2026; preprint, not peer-reviewed)
                                      • Three frontier models hunting the same code agreed on just 1 of 121 findings (Echo, 18 Nov 2026, controlled comparison)
                                      • Curated security rules cut measured risk in AI-generated code by up to 70% (Secure Code Warrior, 17 Nov 2026, AI Trust Index wave 2)
                                      • Deterministic SAST and AI code review each miss what the other catches — combined they reach 101 of 108 attacker-reachable flaws (Snyk, arXiv:2610.38617, submitted 28 Oct 2026; preprint, not peer-reviewed)
                                      • Roughly half of test-passing AI code is vulnerable (SecureVibeBench, Chen et al., ACL 2026, peer-reviewed)
                                      • A green test suite on AI-written code is weak evidence of security (A.S.E 2.0, arXiv:2611.06294, Nov 2026; preprint)
                                      • Fixed vulnerabilities come back when agents rebuild the feature (BugLedger Bench, results 2 Nov 2026)
                                      • Agentic review misses 22–68% of vulnerability-introducing changes (SecPRBench, arXiv:2610.33509, 24 Oct 2026; preprint)
                                      • 96% don't fully trust AI output, only 48% always review it (Sonar, 2026 State of Code survey, 1,100+ developers)

                                      BEDROCK 01 // TECH SURGES Carlota Perez (2002)
                                      Installation, Turning Point & Deployment

                                      Perez’s Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital defines five phases (irruption, frenzy, turning point, synergy, maturity) across two periods (installation and deployment). The turning point is a process of contextual and regulatory recomposition—a pendulum swing toward collective well-being—not a bounded phase, and its outcome is not guaranteed.

                                      Tool mapping: The 4 zones merge irruption+frenzy and synergy+maturity as this tool’s simplification; numeric % boundaries are illustrative (Perez gives almost no numeric thresholds).
                                      BEDROCK 02 // PRODUCTIVITY PARADOX Robert Solow (12 Jul 1987)
                                      “You can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics.”

                                      Quoted from Solow’s New York Times Book Review essay (12 July 1987). Solow’s apparatus measures the aggregate Total Factor Productivity (TFP) residual—not corporate profit margins. Extending the paradox to firm-level P&L is this tool’s diagnostic framing, licensed by the paradox being the signature of installation (§II.2.5).

                                      Maps to: Aggregate productivity residual (TFP) — the dual-curve gap is the tool’s visualisation of the paradox, not Solow’s measurement.
                                      COMPLEMENTARY 01 // J-CURVE Brynjolfsson, Rock & Syverson (WP 2018 / AEJ 2021)
                                      Intangible Complementary Investment Drag

                                      Brynjolfsson’s J-curve models measured Total Factor Productivity being understated during intangible build-out (process redesign, guardrails, verification) and overstated during harvest. It is a model, not a measurement—it gives no P&L trough depth and cannot tell you where on the J you are.

                                      Tool mapping: The ~25% trough shown on Line B is this tool’s schematic quantification, governed by β ≈ 2.5× (code domain only).
