Confluence<1>'s work has included the United Nations Situation Room.

Confluence<1> has always taken the problems others would not or could not — the systems where failure is not an option, where the requirements are the hardest in the industry, and where the stakes are the highest. The report's chief editor is Yorgo Bougás, Confluence<1>'s founder and managing partner. Before Confluence<1>, Yorgo spent 9 years at Sanford C. Bernstein. Bernstein was a boutique, research-driven investment manager — founded in 1967 as an investment manager and broker-dealer, it took discretionary accounts from the start and became well known on the strength of its research. Yorgo's contribution was building the systems that let Bernstein scale that business — separately managed accounts with full tax awareness, daily portfolio optimization, and minimal need for hands-on portfolio managers. Those systems are still at the core of Bernstein's SMA business: in their current form, they autonomously manage $235 billion across roughly 75,000 to 100,000+ separately managed accounts, as of 2026 (within AllianceBernstein, which manages nearly $1 trillion in total assets). After having built those systems first on mainframes, then on VAX/DEC stations, and then on PCs, Yorgo also spent some time in Bernstein's Equity Strategy research department, working with the firm's Chief Investment Strategist, Michael Goldstein.

Responsible, applicable, actionable research, from the perspective of critical systems, is in the Confluence<1> DNA.

## What this report is

CI SITREP tracks the state of the art in Created Intelligence in code and finance: what AI code and agents mean for your P&L; the agentic staff doing the work; certifying the code already in your codebase; and sanitizing AI-generated code before it is committed. It is a knowledge base, updated daily, for people who carry profit-and-loss responsibility and need to stay current to make sound budget decisions. Confluence<1> builds mission-critical systems for the finance industry; in this space, it builds sanitizing tools for clients and provides sanitizing services, as well as advising on and deploying others'.

Every article carries a confidence rating — a dots score for how much confidence you can place in its finding. See the legend on the main page for what each level means.

## How this is made

CI SITREP is a Right-Time Situational Awareness Tool, published by Confluence<1>. It exists so that we only need to monitor one SITREP — and now we're letting you in on it too.

It takes a lot to put together. Since tracking began on 12 November we have reviewed 559 sources and kept 71 of them — a 12.7% acceptance rate. Most of what we review doesn't clear the bar. The result is 122 findings, every one confidence-rated (48% Established or Corroborated), drawn from 291 source links, every one verified live. Before anything publishes, it passes 59 automated checks. The work is done by 6 CII's (AI agents) — research, editing, publishing — working under an editing team with 39 years of cumulative experience building critical systems for finance.

**How we choose what to look at.** There is no fixed list of sources to defend. The radar runs fresh every morning across the four sections — PNL, CIC (Agentic Staff), Codebase Certification, Code Sanitizing Pre-commit — and everything found is judged against one bar: would it change what our clients need to do? Vetting happens on the fly, at intake. Reader suggestions are open: write to ci-sitrep@confluence1.com.

**What it is for.** Advising the finance industry on critical technologies and building the critical systems that they depend on. It is designed to be useful to elite operators — people with profit-and-loss responsibility who need the information that sits behind the good calls - and nothing they don't need.
